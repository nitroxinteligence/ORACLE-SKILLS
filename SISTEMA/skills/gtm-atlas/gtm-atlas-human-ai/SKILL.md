# GTM Atlas Human-AI Operations

Maximize velocity with depth: let AI absorb research, context assembly, administration, and repeatable execution so one informed human can hold more of the customer relationship.

## Workflow

1. Start from the company's unique offer, customer, and bespoke playbook. Named sales methodologies can train behaviors but do not define the motion.
2. Inventory the work across prospecting, research, preparation, discovery, solution design, follow-up, CRM maintenance, reporting, adoption, and renewal.
3. Mark tasks by their need for curiosity, trust, negotiation, empathy, judgment, and accountability.
4. Assign AI to deep research, account prioritization, knowledge retrieval, notes, field updates, reporting, task creation, and mass customization when evidence and permissions support it.
5. Keep humans responsible for thoughtful discovery, value articulation, sensitive decisions, commitments, and conversations where relationship quality changes the outcome.
6. Design one augmented owner to retain continuity across the lifecycle where possible. Give that person just-in-time technical, commercial, and customer context.
7. Define review and escalation for AI output, including source grounding, confidence, write permissions, and rollback for consequential actions.
8. Measure customer-facing live time, effective AI delegation, customer outcomes, and coaching evidence from real conversations.
9. Use conversation review to improve human discovery and value articulation; use workflow telemetry to improve the AI system.

## Guardrails

- Do not automate a task solely because a model can generate plausible output.
- Never let automation make unauthorized commitments or silently write uncertain facts to the CRM.
- Preserve a clear accountable human for each customer and decision.
- Optimize for stronger human performance and customer experience, not headcount reduction as an isolated goal.

## Output

Return: work inventory, human/AI ownership matrix, augmented-role design, context system, approval and escalation rules, metrics, coaching loop, implementation phases, and risks.
