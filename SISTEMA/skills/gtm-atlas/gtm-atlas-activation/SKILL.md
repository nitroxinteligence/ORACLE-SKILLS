# GTM Atlas Activation

Define activation as a meaningful customer outcome that establishes repeat behavior and predicts a downstream business result.

## Workflow

1. Name the product outcome the user came to achieve and the business outcome it should predict.
2. Map setup, first value, repeated value, and habit as separate stages.
3. Choose one simple activation metric, then write the context that prevents literal or harmful optimization.
4. For AI products, separate fascination with model output from completion of the product job. Measure whether curiosity becomes repeat use and value.
5. Identify healthy friction needed for context, intent, quality, safety, or collaboration. Remove friction that does not improve those outcomes.
6. Cluster secondary aha moments by user segment and job. Use them for personalization, education, and referral without diluting the core workflow.
7. Compare activation cohorts with retention and revenue. Fix product leakage before adding lifecycle messages to compensate for it.
8. Design experiments with guardrail metrics and a clear expected causal path.

## Guardrails

- A single successful session is not activation without evidence of habit or durable value.
- Correlation alone does not justify forcing users through an event.
- Do not use email automation to hide a weak product experience.
- A product should replace or materially improve a real workflow, not only showcase a novel interaction.

## Output

Return: outcome definition, setup/aha/habit map, primary metric, context and counter-metrics, AI-curiosity diagnostic, segment-specific aha moments, leakage analysis, and experiments.
