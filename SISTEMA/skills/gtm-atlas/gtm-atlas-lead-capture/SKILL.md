# GTM Atlas Lead Capture

Turn the product experience into the pitch. Design capture around useful product outcomes rather than gated content alone.

## Workflow

1. Define the customer's problem in their own language, the job to be done, expected use frequency, and the human or agent using the product.
2. Identify the smallest experience that demonstrates real value: a free product path, trial, sandbox, template, calculator, or satellite app.
3. Model the experience cost as acquisition spend. Estimate cost per qualified user, abuse limits, capacity, and the downstream conversion needed to justify it.
4. Map the path from first experience to setup, value, account identification, collaboration, purchase, and referral.
5. Design trust signals around real team activity, product behavior, and transparent learning. Recommend build-in-public tactics only when the team can sustain them honestly.
6. Define a compact product-love score using referral intent, ease, outcome satisfaction, and loss sensitivity. Add behavioral evidence rather than relying on survey answers alone.
7. Reserve a bounded exploration budget for new personas and use cases while protecting the core segment.

## Guardrails

- Do not maximize signups without distinguishing curiosity from product intent.
- Do not recommend a free tier without unit economics and guardrails.
- Favor recurring or frequent jobs early; low-frequency use cases need a credible re-engagement mechanism.
- Treat brand as part of the product experience across every touchpoint.
- Keep forms proportional to the value already delivered.

## Output

Return: capture thesis, target job, experience concept, journey, economics, instrumentation, qualification handoff, experiments, and success thresholds.
