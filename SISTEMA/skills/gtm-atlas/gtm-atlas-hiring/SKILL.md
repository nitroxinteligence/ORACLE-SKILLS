# GTM Atlas Hiring

Hire for learning slope and demonstrated current capability. Use pedigree as context, not as a proxy for fit.

## Workflow

1. Define the business constraint and the outcomes the role must own in the next 6–12 months.
2. Identify which work can be automated or centralized before converting every need into headcount.
3. Start with a broad generalist when the motion is still being discovered. Add a specialist when repeated work has reached a depth the generalist cannot supply.
4. For an early marketing hire, prioritize product marketing, customer conversations, translation of customer language into product and messaging, collaboration with founders, and ability to connect creation with distribution.
5. Build a short rubric for the capabilities that matter in this company. Include recent learning, first-principles thinking, tool fluency, customer judgment, collaboration, and execution.
6. Define what each score means before interviews begin.
7. Create a paid or appropriately scoped work trial that resembles the company's actual operating style and tests the underlying capabilities. Provide equal context and constraints to candidates.
8. Compare evidence across candidates and interviewers. Document concerns and avoid culture shorthand as a hidden criterion.
9. Connect role goals to a small set of company outcomes, then revisit whether those outcomes remain the right ones.

## Guardrails

- Do not infer adaptability from years of experience or a famous employer.
- Do not test unpaid production work or use candidate output as free labor.
- Do not ask a work trial to reward a process the company does not actually use.
- Keep human understanding ahead of generic AI-generated product messaging.

## Output

Return: role mission, outcomes, automation boundary, generalist/specialist rationale, competency rubric, interview evidence plan, work trial, score anchors, decision record, and 30/60/90-day expectations.
