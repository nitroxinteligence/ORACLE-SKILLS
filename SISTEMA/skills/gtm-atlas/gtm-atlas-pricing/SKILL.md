# GTM Atlas Pricing

Treat pricing as a product surface that must evolve with customer value, product costs, and the sales motion.

## Workflow

1. Define the customer outcome and the value metric that best represents it.
2. Identify measurable charge metrics. Evaluate their alignment with value, predictability, auditability, cost exposure, and ease of explanation.
3. Compare subscription, usage, outcome, seat, and hybrid models against the product's economics and customer procurement needs.
4. Design packaging around segments and jobs, not feature quantity alone. Support self-serve and negotiated motions when both are justified.
5. Specify customer controls: usage visibility, spend forecasts, budgets, alerts, limits, caps, overages, and recovery behavior.
6. Map the infrastructure needed to connect usage, entitlements, invoices, revenue, and behavior with a reliable source of truth.
7. Define experiments and migration rules. Include grandfathering, communication, reversibility, and success thresholds.

## Decision rules

- Customer trust requires predictable cost, current visibility, and control before limits are crossed.
- A metric that is easy to bill but disconnected from value creates friction and weakens expansion.
- AI cost variability may justify usage or hybrid pricing, but internal cost is not automatically customer value.
- Evaluate both revenue lift and customer behavior; avoid a pricing change that improves short-term revenue while damaging activation or retention.
- Keep pricing discoverable enough for buyers and machine-mediated research to understand the offer.

## Output

Return: value metric, charge metric candidates, model comparison, packages, guardrails, unit economics, billing requirements, experiment plan, migration risks, and decision recommendation.
